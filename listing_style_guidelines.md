# Real Estate Listing Style Guidelines

## Purpose

These guidelines define the writing style for property listing descriptions.

## Tone

Use a:

- Professional
- Natural
- Buyer-focused
- Positive
- Clear
- Concise

tone.

## Writing Principles

1. Focus only on verified property information.
2. Highlight genuine benefits supported by the supplied facts.
3. Use natural language rather than exaggerated marketing language.
4. Make the description easy for prospective buyers to understand.
5. Keep the narrative lifestyle-focused while remaining factual.
6. Do not invent neighborhood information.
7. Do not create unsupported property benefits.

## Avoid Unsupported Claims

Do not introduce claims about:

- Schools
- Restaurants
- Shopping
- Transportation
- Parks
- Views
- Community amenities
- Investment potential
- Rental income
- Property appreciation

unless explicitly provided.

## Avoid Excessive Clichés

Avoid phrases such as:

- Stunning
- Gorgeous
- Once-in-a-lifetime opportunity
- Dream home
- Unparalleled
- Spectacular
- Best property in the area
- Incredible investment

unless such language is directly supported and genuinely appropriate.

Prefer specific factual descriptions.

## Positive Framing

When a property requires renovation, describe the opportunity positively without concealing the condition.

Example:

Input:

"Needs renovation."

Preferred approach:

"An exciting opportunity to customize and add value."

Do not state or imply that renovation has already occurred.

## Narrative Guidelines

The narrative must:

- Contain exactly two paragraphs.
- Remain under 250 words.
- Use only provided facts.
- Focus on buyer-relevant benefits.
- Avoid fabricated details.

## Call-to-Action Guidelines

The CTA should encourage a prospective buyer to schedule a viewing or tour.

Examples of acceptable CTA intent:

- Schedule a tour to explore the property in person.
- Contact us to arrange a viewing.
- Schedule a viewing to see the property's features firsthand.
